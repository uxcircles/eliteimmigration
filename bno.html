BN(O) visas, from entry clearance to a British passport

Specialist advice and representation for Hong Kong BN(O) status holders and their families, in English, Cantonese and Mandarin.

Why BN(O) applications are refused

The route is meant to be flexible, but applications are often delayed or refused because of misunderstandings about eligibility, family relationships or documents. We make sure each application is assessed, evidenced and presented in line with the Immigration Rules and Home Office guidance.

Who we act for

  • BN(O) status holders applying from overseas or in the UK
  • Spouses, partners and dependent children
  • Adult children and other household members under the BN(O) Household Member route
  • Families with complex living arrangements or dependency histories

What we do

  • Assess BN(O) status and family eligibility
  • Advise on the best route and timing
  • Prepare and submit entry clearance and in-country applications
  • Evidence household membership, dependency and residence
  • Switching, extensions and continuous residence
  • ILR and British citizenship
  • Advice after a refusal, including administrative review

The 2 November 2026 announcement

BN(O) holders keep the 5-year route to settlement while most other routes move to a 10-year baseline. If you are on another visa, we can tell you whether switching into the BN(O) route is worth it.
